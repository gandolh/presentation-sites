## Page structure

A single scrolling page, in the order a prospective client actually decides:

1. **Hero** — the room, the name, and one line of what this place is.
2. **Services** — what can be booked, with prices where they are fixed.
3. **Gallery** — the work itself. This is the section that sells; everything
   else supports it.
4. **About** — who Ana is. Short, because the gallery has already made the case.
5. **Contact and location** — the practical close: where, when, and how to book.

The order is a claim about the reader: someone choosing a nail salon is looking
at *work* first and reading second. Moving the gallery below the fold would be
inverting that.

## The design decisions worth knowing

The full system is in [the design system](/wiki/design/), captured from the live
tokens rather than proposed ahead of them — **"this is the committed brand, not a
proposal"**, and new work composes within it.

Three rules are easy to break by accident and are worth stating here:

- **Colour strategy is restrained.** Tinted near-white surfaces carry the page, a
  single blush brand colour does the work, and warmth comes from the surface
  tint, the serif face and the photography — not from saturation.
- **Gold is an accent, never a fill.** At most ~10% of any surface: dividers,
  focus rings, small flourishes. Not a background, not gradient text.
- **Blush is the action colour.** Buttons, selection, link hover.

Explicitly out: gradient text, side-stripe borders, glassmorphism.
